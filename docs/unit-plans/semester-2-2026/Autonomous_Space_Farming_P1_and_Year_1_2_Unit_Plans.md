# Autonomous Space Farming

**Detailed Technologies Unit Plans:** Prep/Year 1 and Year 1/2  
**Semester:** Semester 2, 2026  
**Technologies subject:** Digital Technologies  
**Australian Curriculum:** Version 9.0  
**Project connection:** Advanced Manufacturing / Space Farming Showcase  

## Core idea

Students explore how autonomous robots could help astronauts grow food on the Moon, Mars or another planet. Students learn that digital systems follow instructions and that digital solutions can meet real user needs.

## Planning note

The Prep/Year 1 and Year 1/2 units are intentionally connected but assessed differently.

Prep students focus on Foundation Digital Technologies expectations through familiar digital systems, simple movement sequences, pictures, symbols and oral explanations.

Year 1 and Year 2 students work towards the Year 2 Digital Technologies achievement standard, including simple digital solutions, data representation, basic algorithms, safe digital use and collaboration.

# Unit 1: Prep/Year 1

## Autonomous Space Farming: Robot Farmers on the Moon

**Year level:** Prep/Year 1 multi-age class  
**Learning area:** Technologies  
**Technologies subject:** Digital Technologies  
**Duration:** 18 weeks, 1 lesson per week  
**Main tools:** LEGO WeDo 2.0, Scratch Jr, Bee-Bots or similar floor robots, optional guided Minecraft Education exploration  
**Showcase product:** Students share a simple robot farming mission, visual farm map and explanation of how robot farmers could help astronauts grow food away from Earth.

## Unit rationale

Students investigate the idea that people living on the Moon or another planet would need food, shelter, water and safe places to work. They learn that robots can follow instructions to complete jobs that may be dangerous, repetitive or difficult for people. Through hands-on activities with LEGO WeDo, Scratch Jr and simple programmable robots, students learn that digital systems can be used for a purpose and that instructions need to be placed in the correct order.

For Prep students, the focus is on becoming familiar with digital systems, using them for a simple purpose, representing data with pictures and symbols and identifying examples of personal data. Prep students are not assessed on Minecraft programming. Minecraft is used only as a guided exploration or teacher demonstration where appropriate.

For Year 1 students, the same learning experiences are extended so students explain how a simple digital solution helps known users, follow and describe basic algorithms, access digital systems with assistance and collaborate safely.

## Prep/Year 1 learning sequence

1. What do astronauts need?
2. Meet a digital system
3. LEGO WeDo: motor and movement
4. LEGO WeDo: speed and change
5. Scratch Jr: move a robot
6. Scratch Jr: path to the greenhouse
7. Bee-Bot space farm mission
8. Farm data with pictures and symbols
9. Personal data and safe digital use
10. Design a robot farmer
11. Optional Minecraft exploration
12. Plan my robot mission
13. Build or create the mission
14. Test and improve
15. Prepare showcase explanation
16. Final assessment work time
17. Present to peers
18. Showcase and reflection

## Prep/Year 1 assessment task

**Task title:** Robot Farmer Mission

**Scenario:** Astronauts are living on the Moon. They need help growing food. Students create a robot farming mission that shows how a robot can help.

### Prep evidence

- A picture or map of a space farm
- A simple robot movement task using Bee-Bot, Scratch Jr or teacher-supported digital tool
- Picture or symbol sequence or teacher-scribed plan
- Oral explanation of what the robot does
- Evidence of recognising personal data, such as name, photo, avatar or login

### Year 1 evidence

- A labelled robot farmer design
- A simple algorithm using Scratch Jr, Bee-Bot or guided Minecraft exploration
- Farm map or data display
- Ordered algorithm steps
- Testing evidence and one improvement
- Explanation of how the robot helps astronauts

## Prep A–E rubric criteria

- Using digital systems
- Representing data
- Following steps
- Personal data
- Explaining the solution

## Year 1 A–E rubric criteria

- User need
- Algorithm
- Data representation
- Digital tool use
- Collaboration and safety

# Unit 2: Year 1/2

## Autonomous Space Farming: Minecraft Agent Farmer Mission

**Year level:** Year 1/2  
**Learning area:** Technologies  
**Technologies subject:** Digital Technologies  
**Duration:** 18 weeks, 1 lesson per week  
**Main tools:** Minecraft Education, Agent programming, Scratch Jr or MakeCode-style block coding, Bee-Bots for early unplugged/physical practice  
**Showcase product:** Students present a Minecraft Education Moon or Mars farm where an Agent follows an algorithm to prepare a farm area and plant crops.

## Unit rationale

This unit gives Year 1/2 students a direct Digital Technologies programming pathway. Students begin with movement algorithms, then progress to programming an Agent in Minecraft Education to complete a useful farming task. The focus is not just building in Minecraft; the Technologies learning is about designing a simple digital solution, creating and describing algorithms, representing farm data and explaining how the solution meets a need for known users.

Future space settlements may use autonomous robots to build habitats, prepare soil, plant seeds, water crops and monitor food production. Students learn that digital technologies can automate tasks and solve human problems.

## Achievement standard focus

By the end of Year 2, students show how simple digital solutions meet a need for known users. Students represent and process data in different ways. They follow and describe basic algorithms involving a sequence of steps and branching. With assistance, students access and use digital systems for a purpose. They use the basic features of common digital tools to create, locate and share content and to collaborate, following agreed behaviours. Students recognise that digital tools may store their personal data online.

## Year 1/2 learning sequence

1. The space farming problem
2. Algorithms are instructions
3. Scratch Jr or Bee-Bot movement mission
4. Debugging and improving movement
5. Representing farm data
6. Personal data and digital responsibility
7. Meet the Minecraft Agent
8. Agent movement challenge
9. Repeating rows
10. Preparing a 2 x 2 farm patch
11. Preparing a 4 x 4 farm
12. Planting crops
13. Branching: making a choice
14. Test and debug the farming algorithm
15. Improve farm layout and data display
16. Prepare showcase explanation
17. Peer presentation and feedback
18. Showcase and reflection

## Year 1/2 assessment task

**Task title:** Minecraft Agent Farmer Mission

Students create a Minecraft Agent farming solution for astronauts who need food in a space settlement. Evidence should include:

- User needs chart
- Farm map
- Algorithm plan
- Agent program or screenshots
- Crop/farm data table
- Testing and debugging evidence
- Reflection and showcase explanation

## Year 1/2 A–E rubric criteria

- Digital solution and user need
- Algorithm sequence and branching
- Data representation and processing
- Digital tool use
- Testing and debugging
- Collaboration, safety and personal data

# Year 4–6 Naturally Brave: Disaster-Ready Design

**Semester:** Semester 2, 2026  
**Learning area:** Technologies  
**Subject:** Design Technologies  
**Class:** Year 4/5/6  
**Duration:** 18 weeks, 1 lesson per week  
**Technologies contexts:** Engineering principles and systems; Materials and technologies specialisations  
**Authentic partner:** LifeFlight Toowoomba  
**Showcase outcome:** Students present a LifeFlight-inspired disaster-ready design solution at the November showcase.

## Unit overview

Students investigate natural disasters, community needs and emergency response contexts. They use design thinking, technical drawing, 3D modelling, physical prototyping and optional Micro:bit or sensor integration to design a practical disaster-ready solution inspired by the LifeFlight NVG protective cap and storage case challenge.

The major authentic design problem is based on the need for improved protective caps and/or a storage insert or case for F4949-1 night vision goggles and accessories. The solution should secure protective caps, protect equipment from bumps and scratches, organise goggles and accessories, show when items are missing, remain durable, lightweight and easy to clean and be simple enough to manufacture using 3D printing or similar prototyping methods.

## Major design challenge

How might we design a protective cap, storage insert or storage case system that helps LifeFlight protect, organise and quickly check night vision goggles and related equipment during emergency response work?

## Possible student design outcomes

- 3D printed protective caps for night vision goggles
- A custom storage insert
- A labelled accessory tray
- A missing-item indicator system
- A Micro:bit or simple sensor feature to support checking, locating or monitoring equipment
- A physical prototype made from cardboard, foam, timber, 3D printed parts or mixed materials
- A technical drawing, annotated design file and evaluation report

## 18 week learning sequence

1. Unit launch: Naturally Brave and disaster response
2. LifeFlight and the authentic design problem
3. Empathise: user needs and emergency context
4. Investigate materials, forces and protection
5. Define the design criteria
6. Ideate: rapid concept generation
7. Technical drawing and graphical communication
8. Makers Empire or CAD modelling
9. Prototype planning
10. Low-fidelity prototyping
11. Testing round 1: fit, protection and usability
12. Micro:bit or sensor integration
13. Prototype refinement
14. Final prototype construction
15. Testing round 2 and final evaluation
16. Showcase communication and display preparation
17. Practice presentation and peer feedback
18. Final showcase and reflection

6. Showcase communication and reflection
